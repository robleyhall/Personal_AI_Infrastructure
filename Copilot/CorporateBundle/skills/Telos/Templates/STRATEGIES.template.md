# Strategies

*Template — replace with how you actually work. Strategies are durable; tactics change.*

## On accounts

- **Start with the customer's business clock, not the platform's feature
  clock.** Pace recommendations to what they can operationalize.
- **Always name the trade-off.** Every recommendation has a second-order
  cost — write it down where the customer will see it later.
- **Design for the next hand-off, not the current demo.** The engagement
  ends; the operating team stays.

## On solutions

- **Reversible first, irreversible last.** Prefer designs that let the
  customer roll back without rebuilding.
- **Governance and observability are not Phase 2.** If they are, cut scope
  until they can be Phase 1.
- **No novel security.** Use the platform's native controls unless the
  customer has a documented reason and a compensating plan.

## On my own depth

- **One platform per quarter, two hours per week minimum.** Hands-on, not
  passive reading.
- **Write what I learn in my own words.** Lessons get captured in
  `~/.pai/MEMORY/LEARNING/` as I go, not at the end.
- **Prune capabilities I'm not using.** Claim less; know more.

## On leverage

- **Templates over bespoke decks.** If I'm making the same slide twice, it
  becomes a template.
- **Design docs over slide decks** whenever the audience allows it.
- **Feed the org.** Patterns, failure modes, and wins go to the CSA
  community, not just to one account.

## On saying no

- I say no to engagements where the business case doesn't support the
  architecture effort.
- I say no to "one more meeting" that substitutes for a decision.
- I say no when I can't defend the recommendation on first principles.
