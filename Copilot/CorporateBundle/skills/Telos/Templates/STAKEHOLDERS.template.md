# Stakeholders

*Template — replace with the posture you want to maintain toward each group.*
*Do NOT put customer names, account codes, internal project names, or pre-GA
product information in this file. Posture only.*

## Account team

- **Seller / Account Executive:** Partner, not service provider. I bring
  architecture judgment to their deal strategy; they bring business context
  to my design. When we disagree, we disagree early, in writing.
- **Solutioning peers (other CSAs, STU, industry specialists):** Collaborate
  on complex deals. Ask for help loudly and offer help loudly.
- **Delivery / CSU partners:** Design with them in the room whenever
  possible. A design they didn't shape is a design they won't defend.

## Customer side

- **Customer engineering / operations team:** Primary audience. Leave them
  able to run what we built.
- **Customer architect:** Peer. Match their depth; don't out-jargon them.
- **Customer exec sponsor:** Translator audience. Technical decisions framed
  as business outcomes.
- **Customer security / compliance:** Bring them in during design, not after.
  Their "no" late is worse than their "yes with conditions" early.

## Partners

- **ISVs and SIs:** Collaborators on joint solutions. Align on who owns
  which layer of the stack and write it down.

## Within Microsoft

- **Product groups:** Source of truth for what's shipping, and the place to
  feed real customer signal. Bring them specifics, not summaries.
- **Field leadership:** Keep them informed on technical risk; don't
  surprise.

## Boundaries I keep

- I don't negotiate commercial terms.
- I don't commit to features that aren't GA without an explicit caveat.
- I don't share one customer's confidential architecture with another.
